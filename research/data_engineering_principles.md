# Data Engineering Principles Across the Data Lifecycle: Evidence Review

Status: research phase only. This document contains no schemas and no implementation. It feeds a later technology-independent Database Schema Design and Data Engineering Standards Framework for ERP, financial, SaaS and data engineering systems.

Compiled 2026-11-08 from the project research notes (topic files 04, 05, 08, 13, 15, with a targeted check of 10, and CORRECTIONS.md, which overrides them). Updated 2026-11-08 from notes 20, 21 and 22 and `verification_results.json`. No web research was done for this document and no source outside the notes is cited. The sources were opened by the note authors on or about 2026-11-07.

Companion document: `data_pipeline_architecture.md` (ETL and ELT, streaming, Kafka, CDC, table formats, orchestration, and requirements on source schemas).

## 0. Reading conventions

### 0.1 Labels

The label on each principle is the **proposed framework position**. It may be stronger or weaker than the source's wording, and the Basis tag makes the difference visible.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MANDATORY** | A precondition of correctness, safety or compliance in the stated scope. |
| **RECOMMENDED** | Default practice in the stated scope. Deviation needs a stated reason. |
| **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** | A trade-off to select per workload. |

| Basis tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[S]** | The cited source states the rule. |
| **[S-weaker]** | The source recommends it, and the framework proposes to raise it. |
| **[I]** | Inference or framework decision drawn from cited mechanisms. |

### 0.2 Workload scope tags

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **OLTP** | Transactional systems of record: ERP, ledgers, SaaS application databases. |
| **OLAP** | Analytical query workloads on analytical engines generally. |
| **DW** | Modeled data warehouses: staging, integration, dimensional presentation. |
| **Lakehouse** | Open table formats or lakehouse platforms on object storage. |
| **Streaming** | Event logs and stream processors. |
| **Financial** | An overlay on the above, where amounts must tie and periods close. |
| **All** | Every scope. Used only when the evidence truly spans them. |

The distinction matters most for integrity. The same declaration, such as PRIMARY KEY, is an enforced guarantee in an OLTP database and documentation-only metadata on a standard cloud warehouse table (section 4.2).

### 0.3 Citation and quotation policy

- Citations read as Title, URL, then date or n.d., catalog ID where one exists, and flags. `vendor` marks a commercial vendor's statement about its own product or market. A missing catalog ID means the URL is in the notes without an exact catalog match.
- Most web pages were read through a summarizing fetch tool. **Statements from web pages are paraphrases** unless in quotation marks.
- Quotation marks appear for text recorded as PDF-extracted (NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1, OPS-17), text extracted directly by curl (note 20), and exact or corrected wording confirmed in note 22 (DORA, Braintree 2019, GitLab, Evolutionary Database Design, strong_migrations, Testcontainers). Wording note 22 found to differ uses only the corrected text.
- **[WF]** marks a finding read only through WebFetch. It stays paraphrase and needs re-checking against the page before reliance.
- ISO standards are paywalled. Only scope statements are used; clause content is not asserted.

## 2. Stage: Data Modeling

### 2.1 Naming and semantics

- **MOD-01. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All.** Adopt a documented naming convention. ISO/IEC 11179-5 sets out principles and rules for developing naming conventions in metadata registries: [ISO/IEC 11179-5:2015 (IEC webstore)](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/21986) (2015-03-18; GOV-08; scope statement only). Basis [S], scope statement only. Clause rules of the published standard were not visible. A partly downloaded 2004 committee draft says the scope "of each naming convention represented in the MDR shall be documented" and likewise authority: [ISO/IEC FCD 11179-5 committee draft](https://www.jtc1sc32.org/doc/N1051-1100/32N1073T-FCD11179-5_doc) (2004-01-09; THIN-08; partly downloaded draft). That is draft text for registries, not the published standard.
- **MOD-02. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: All.** The object-class, property and representation-term form is associated with 11179-5, but it is not visible in the official abstract. It is confirmed through secondary sources and the informative Annex A of the 2004 draft ([ISO/IEC FCD 11179-5 committee draft](https://www.jtc1sc32.org/doc/N1051-1100/32N1073T-FCD11179-5_doc) (2004-01-09; THIN-08; partly downloaded draft)), not published text. Readability objections are recorded in the notes. Basis [I]. Describe a convention as aligned with 11179-5, not required by it.
- **MOD-03. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW.** Use consistent field names across models, business rather than source terminology, and typed suffixes. dbt recommends snake_case, `<object>_id` keys, `is_` or `has_` booleans and UTC `_at` timestamps: [How we style our dbt models](https://docs.getdbt.com/best-practices/how-we-style/1-how-we-style-our-dbt-models) (2026-09-10; LAKE-16; vendor). Basis [S], vendor-originated, and the names are generic enough to reuse outside dbt.
- **MOD-04. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: All.** Score model quality with a structured instrument, such as the Data Model Scorecard's ten categories. The industry-standard wording is the publisher's claim and not a standards body's: [Technics Publications: Data Model Scorecard](https://technicspub.com/data-model-scorecard/) (n.d.; GOV-32). Basis [S].
- **MOD-05. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: All.** Keep logical modeling independent of physical design. FIPS 184 described an application-independent view transformable into a physical design: [NIST: FIPS PUB 184](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/FIPS/fipspub184.pdf) (1993-12-21; GOV-27). It has been reported withdrawn in 2008, on a search-returned source, so it is historical. Basis [S] for the objective.

### 2.2 Reference data

- **MOD-06. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP, DW.** Use maintained code lists, such as ISO 3166 and ISO 4217, instead of inventing codes. ISO permits free use of those lists: [ISO 3166: Country Codes](https://www.iso.org/iso-3166-country-codes.html) (n.d.; GOV-19; UNVERIFIED: search-returned; fetch 403). SIX publishes ISO 4217 lists including a minor-unit field: [SIX: Data Standards (ISO 4217)](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/financial-information/market-reference-data/data-standards.html) (n.d.; GOV-20). Basis [S].
- **MOD-07. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP, DW, Financial.** Take currency scale from data, not constants. Lists change: Bulgaria adopted the euro effective 2026-01-01, and BGN moved to the historical list: [SIX: Data Standards (ISO 4217)](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/financial-information/market-reference-data/data-standards.html) (n.d.; GOV-20). Basis [S] for changes, [I] for design.
- **MOD-08. MANDATORY. Scope: Financial, in regulated reporting contexts GLEIF lists.** Use the LEI for legal-entity identification where mandated (CFTC 2013, MiFID II 2018, RBI 2021, Korea FSC 2023, Japan FSA 2024): [GLEIF: The Legal Entity Identifier](https://www.gleif.org/en/organizational-identity/lei-vlei/the-legal-entity-identifier-lei) (n.d.; GOV-24). The official replacement page (note 22) says the LEI is "a unique 20-character alphanumeric code" and "is based on the global ISO 17442 standard", and does not give character-position structure. Basis [S]; the list of mandates was taken from the earlier page, so confirm each mandate against the regulator before relying on it.
- **MOD-09. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All, for interchange only.** Exchange date and time as ISO 8601-1 representations. Its scope is representations for information interchange, not storage types: [ISO/TC 154: ISO 8601-1:2019](https://isotc154.org/standards/iso-8601-1-2019) (2019-03-25; GOV-21). Basis [S].

### 2.3 Design-time metadata

- **MOD-10. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse, and OLTP tables shared beyond the owning team.** Every table has an owner and a description at creation. Basis [I]: dbt requires an owner on exposures ([dbt: Add exposures to your DAG](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/exposures) (2026-11-03; vendor)), ODCS includes team and roles ([Open Data Contract Standard (latest)](https://bitol-io.github.io/open-data-contract-standard/latest/) (n.d.; PIPE-20)), and the DGI framework names accountabilities ([DGI: Framework Components](https://datagovernance.com/the-dgi-data-governance-framework/dgi-data-governance-framework-components/) (n.d.; GOV-18)). None mandates this for every table.
- **MOD-11. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All.** Keep descriptions in the schema artifact, using `COMMENT ON` or model YAML. Never put sensitive values in comments: any connected user can see them in PostgreSQL: [PostgreSQL 18: COMMENT](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-comment.html) (n.d.; DQ-25). Basis [S] for the warning, [I] for the placement.
- **MOD-12. MANDATORY. Scope: All data classified as personal or confidential.** Classify columns containing such data at design time, because masking and tag-driven policy depend on the classification ([Tag-based masking policies](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tag-based-masking-policies) (n.d.; vendor), [Row filters and column masks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/tables/row-and-column-filters) (2026-11-06; LAKE-04; vendor)). Basis [I].

## 3. Stage: Schema Design and schema change

### 3.1 Evolutionary discipline

- **CHG-01. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP; applies to DW DDL where migrations are used.** Every schema change is a small, version-controlled, tool-applied script tracked in a history table. Evolutionary Database Design has the section heading "All database artifacts are version controlled with application code" and describes "representing every change to the database as a database migration script"; DORA says teams "store their database changes as scripts in version control and manage these changes in the same way they manage production application changes". Basis [S]: [Evolutionary Database Design](https://martinfowler.com/articles/evodb.html) (2016-05; OPS-01), [DORA: Database change management](https://dora.dev/capabilities/database-change-management/) (n.d.; OPS-15).
- **CHG-02. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP under continuous delivery.** Incompatible changes use parallel change (expand, migrate, contract), so old and new forms coexist in a transition period. The cost is supporting two versions, and finishing takes discipline: [ParallelChange](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ParallelChange.html) (2014-05-13; OPS-02), [Evolutionary Database Design](https://martinfowler.com/articles/evodb.html) (2016-05; OPS-01). Basis [S]. Sato credits Kerievsky (2006) for first documenting the pattern: [ParallelChange](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ParallelChange.html) (2014-05-13; OPS-02).
- **CHG-03. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP.** Every change must be deployable while the previous application version runs. Basis [I]: the note authors' meta-rule, consistent with [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04) and [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07).
- **CHG-04. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP, DW.** Never edit a migration already applied in a permanent environment: roll forward. Basis [S]: [Flyway: Versioned migrations](https://documentation.red-gate.com/flyway/flyway-concepts/migrations/versioned-migrations) (2024-12-17, updated 2024-12-23; OPS-10; vendor), [Rails 8.1: Active Record Migrations](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/active_record_migrations.html) (n.d., v8.1.4.1).
- **CHG-05. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP, where tools generate migrations.** Review every generated migration before merge. Alembic says manual review is always necessary, and it cannot detect renames, reporting them as add and drop: [Alembic: Auto Generating Migrations](https://alembic.sqlalchemy.org/en/latest/autogenerate.html) (n.d., Alembic 1.20.1; OPS-12). Basis [S] for Alembic, [I] for generalizing.
- **CHG-06. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP, Financial.** Keep data changes separate from schema changes. See conflict C4 in section 11. Basis [S] with disagreement: [Rails 8.1: Active Record Migrations](https://guides.rubyonrails.org/active_record_migrations.html) (n.d., v8.1.4.1), [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04), [Django 5.2: Migrations](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/migrations/) (n.d., v5.2), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08).
- **CHG-07. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP.** State engine and version on every rule. Several rules change with version, as PostgreSQL 11 did for constant defaults: [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [Safe Operations For High Volume PostgreSQL](https://pgrs.net/2014/05/14/safe-operations-for-high-volume-postgresql/) (2014-05-14; CASE-27). Basis [I].

### 3.2 Lock and duration budgets

- **CHG-08. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP with live traffic.** Set a short lock timeout on migrations. Strong Migrations calls this extremely important: [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08). Basis [S].
- **CHG-09. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP with live traffic.** Define separate budgets for lock hold time and total runtime. These are different quantities:

| Source | Quantity | Value |
|---|---|---|
| [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07) | Exclusive lock hold | "Any exclusive locks acquired on tables or indexes be held for at most ~2 seconds." |
| [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08) | `lock_timeout` example | 10 seconds |
| [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04) | Statement timeout on GitLab.com | 15 seconds |
| [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04) | Regular, post-deployment, concurrent index migrations | 3, 10, 20 minutes |
| [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04) | Any single query, cold caches | Under 1 second |

Basis [S] for values. **The notes state the operators converge on 2 to 10 seconds; the sources are not measuring the same thing, and a statement timeout is not a lock-hold limit.** Values are operator-specific and are not portable defaults.

- **CHG-10. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP, PostgreSQL.** A waiting lock request queues all later queries on that table, so acquire locks with a timeout and retry, after checking for long-running queries: [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04). Basis [S].

### 3.3 Dangerous versus safe operations

Scope of this table: **OLTP only**, primarily PostgreSQL (official docs at 18.6); MySQL where stated. It does not apply to cloud warehouses or table formats, for which see `data_pipeline_architecture.md` section 13. Rule identifiers from Atlas ([Atlas lint analyzers](https://atlasgo.io/lint/analyzers) (n.d.; OPS-13; vendor)) and Squawk ([Squawk rules](https://squawkhq.com/docs/rules) (n.d.; OPS-16)) are shown in code font. All descriptions are paraphrases. Label for the safe sequence column: MANDATORY on high-traffic tables, RECOMMENDED elsewhere, framework position [I] based on the listed operators' own rules.

| Operation | Why it is dangerous | Safe sequence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add nullable column, no default | Brief catalog lock only: safe. `SELECT *` consumers pick up the column | Add directly; use explicit column lists in application code | [Safe Operations For High Volume PostgreSQL](https://pgrs.net/2014/05/14/safe-operations-for-high-volume-postgresql/) (2014-05-14; CASE-27), [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07) |
| Add column with constant default | PostgreSQL 11 and later: metadata only, safe. Before 11, and MySQL expression defaults: rewrite under lock | PG 11+: add directly. Older or MySQL expression: add column, set default separately, backfill in batches | [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [Safe Operations For High Volume PostgreSQL](https://pgrs.net/2014/05/14/safe-operations-for-high-volume-postgresql/) (2014-05-14; CASE-27), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04) |
| Add column with volatile default, identity, stored generated, or constrained domain | Whole table and indexes rewritten | Add without default, set default, batched backfill; or a virtual generated column | [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), Atlas `PG302` |
| Add NOT NULL to existing column | Full scan under ACCESS EXCLUSIVE; application must already write values | Deploy application change, backfill in batches, add CHECK NOT VALID, VALIDATE, SET NOT NULL (scan skipped given the valid CHECK), drop CHECK | [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), [GitLab: NOT NULL constraints](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/not_null_constraints.html) (n.d.), Squawk `adding-not-nullable-field`, Atlas `MF104`, `PG303` |
| Add NOT NULL column to existing table | Rewrite or scan, plus the above | Add nullable, backfill, then NOT NULL sequence. Braintree 2014: new table and dual write for very large tables | [Safe Operations For High Volume PostgreSQL](https://pgrs.net/2014/05/14/safe-operations-for-high-volume-postgresql/) (2014-05-14; CASE-27), Atlas `MF103`, Squawk `adding-required-field` |
| Create index | Blocks writes for the whole build | `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` outside a transaction; if INVALID, drop and retry; on partitioned tables build per partition, then attach | [PostgreSQL CREATE INDEX](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-createindex.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; PERF-10), [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04), Squawk `require-concurrent-index-creation`, Atlas `PG101`, `PG108` |
| Drop index | ACCESS EXCLUSIVE | `DROP INDEX CONCURRENTLY` (not for constraint-backing indexes); name the index explicitly | [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04), Squawk `require-concurrent-index-deletion`, Atlas `PG102` |
| Add UNIQUE constraint | Builds a blocking unique index | Create unique index concurrently, then add the constraint using the index | [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), Atlas `PG105`, `MF101`, Squawk `disallowed-unique-constraint` |
| Add FOREIGN KEY | SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE on both tables plus a validation scan. strong_migrations: "Adding a foreign key blocks writes on both tables." | Add NOT VALID, then VALIDATE (`ALTER TABLE ... ADD FOREIGN KEY ... NOT VALID`, confirmed in Braintree 2019); create tables first and add the FK separately. MySQL has no NOT VALID | [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [GitLab: Avoiding downtime in migrations](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/avoiding_downtime_in_migrations.html) (n.d.; OPS-05), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), Squawk `adding-foreign-key-constraint`, Atlas `PG306` |
| Add CHECK constraint | ACCESS EXCLUSIVE plus scan | NOT VALID, then VALIDATE CONSTRAINT | [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), Squawk `constraint-missing-not-valid` |
| Add EXCLUDE constraint | ACCESS EXCLUSIVE, scan, cannot reuse an index | No safe online alternative documented | [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), Atlas `PG109` |
| Change column type | Table and index rewrite, except binary-coercible changes and some widenings | New column, dual write, batched backfill, swap in a short transaction, drop old | [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), [GitLab: Avoiding downtime in migrations](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/avoiding_downtime_in_migrations.html) (n.d.; OPS-05), Squawk `changing-column-type`, Atlas `PG301` |
| Rename column | Breaks running code; autogenerators see drop and add | Expand and contract: new column, sync, backfill, switch, drop | [GitLab: Avoiding downtime in migrations](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/avoiding_downtime_in_migrations.html) (n.d.; OPS-05), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), [Alembic: Auto Generating Migrations](https://alembic.sqlalchemy.org/en/latest/autogenerate.html) (n.d., Alembic 1.20.1; OPS-12), Atlas `BC102`, Squawk `renaming-column` |
| Rename table | As above | New table with dual write and backfill, or a view under the old name during transition | [GitLab: Avoiding downtime in migrations](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/avoiding_downtime_in_migrations.html) (n.d.; OPS-05), [Evolutionary Database Design](https://martinfowler.com/articles/evodb.html) (2016-05; OPS-01), Atlas `BC101`, Squawk `renaming-table` |
| Drop column | Fast in PostgreSQL, catalog only, but ORM caches and running code break; destructive | Release M: ignore in application. M+1: drop in post-deployment migration. M+2: remove ignore rule | [GitLab: Avoiding downtime in migrations](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/avoiding_downtime_in_migrations.html) (n.d.; OPS-05), [ankane/strong_migrations README](https://github.com/ankane/strong_migrations) (n.d.; OPS-08), [PostgreSQL ALTER TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-altertable.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; OPS-09), Atlas `DS103`, `BC104`, Squawk `ban-drop-column` |
| Drop table | Destructive; FK removal locks both tables | Remove code first; next release drop each FK separately in reverse lock order, then drop the table; verify usage statistics | [GitLab: Avoiding downtime in migrations](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/database/avoiding_downtime_in_migrations.html) (n.d.; OPS-05), [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), Atlas `DS102`, Squawk `ban-drop-table` |

### 3.4 Zero-downtime patterns

- **CHG-11. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP, large-scale data-model migrations.** Dual write, backfill, change reads, change writes, then remove old data, verifying continuously by comparing both paths. Stripe used this approach with Scientist on hundreds of millions of objects: [Online migrations at scale](https://stripe.com/blog/online-migrations) (2017-02-02; OPS-06). Basis [S], one operator.
- **CHG-12. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: OLTP, MySQL large tables.** Select an online tool consciously. pt-online-schema-change uses triggers and will not run on tables with triggers ([pt-online-schema-change docs](https://docs.percona.com/percona-toolkit/pt-online-schema-change.html) (n.d., v3.7.2; vendor)); gh-ost uses the binlog and treats triggers as a source of risk ([github/gh-ost](https://github.com/github/gh-ost) (n.d.; CASE-23)). gh-ost's project is arguing for its own design. Basis [S].
- **CHG-13. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: OLTP on platforms offering it.** Use deploy-request workflows with ghost tables and a revert window: [PlanetScale Safe migrations](https://planetscale.com/docs/concepts/safe-migrations) (n.d.; vendor). Basis [S], vendor-originated.

### 3.5 Rollback

- **CHG-14. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP.** Recovery safety comes from backward-compatible change and tested backups, not from down scripts. Basis [I], from [Flyway: Undo migrations](https://documentation.red-gate.com/fd/undo-migrations-273973334.html) (2024-12-17, updated 2026-05-21; vendor), [Braintree redux gist (jcoleman)](https://gist.github.com/jcoleman/1e6ad1bf8de454c166da94b67537758b) (2019-02-01; OPS-07), with partial agreement from [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04). See conflict C1.
- **CHG-15. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP where down scripts exist.** Test rollback. Liquibase documents deploy-rollback-redeploy testing and notes that complex structural and data changes are hard to roll back; automatic rollback is limited to certain change types and does not cover drops, `sql`, or data operations: [Using Liquibase Rollback](https://docs.liquibase.com/workflows/liquibase-community/using-rollback.html) (updated 2026-08-12; OPS-11; vendor), [Liquibase: Automatic and custom rollbacks](https://docs.liquibase.com/workflows/liquibase-community/automatic-custom-rollbacks.html) (updated 2026-08-12; vendor). Basis [S], vendor.

### 3.6 State-based versus migration-based

- **CHG-16. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: OLTP, DW.** Choice of authoring model. **Conflict.** dbt says most platforms enforce only `not_null` ([dbt: Model contracts](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/mesh/govern/model-contracts) (2026-10-29; LAKE-17; also DQ-03; vendor)), while current Snowflake and Databricks docs state CHECK is also enforced ([Snowflake Constraints overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/sql-reference/constraints-overview) (n.d.; LAKE-09; vendor), [Constraints on Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/tables/constraints) (2026-10-29; LAKE-02; vendor)). dbt's statement concerns its own contract support, and the notes could not map its adapter table cell-by-cell. ### 4.3 Principles

- **VAL-01. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP.** Declare PK, UNIQUE, NOT NULL, FK and CHECK in DDL. Downstream layers cannot retroactively enforce what the source admitted. Basis [I]: the dbt comparison above, and the note authors' reasoning.
- **VAL-02. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Never assume declared PK, FK or UNIQUE is enforced. Basis [S]: [Snowflake Constraints overview](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/sql-reference/constraints-overview) (n.d.; LAKE-09; vendor), [Constraints on Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/tables/constraints) (2026-10-29; LAKE-02; vendor).
- **VAL-03. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Every model has a uniqueness and non-null test on its primary key, and foreign keys get a relationship test where platform enforcement is absent. Basis [S-weaker]: dbt recommends the primary key test: [dbt: Add data tests to your DAG](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/data-tests) (2026-09-16; LAKE-19; vendor). Raised because the platform will otherwise accept duplicates silently.
- **VAL-04. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse, datasets published to consumers.** Use a promotion gate: write out of view, audit, then publish. Netflix (2017) described this write-audit-publish flow and Iceberg documents audit branches: [Scaling Data Quality @ Netflix](https://www.slideshare.net/MichelleFUfford/scaling-data-quality-netflix-76917740) (2017-06-12; DQ-13), [Apache Iceberg: Branching and Tagging](https://iceberg.apache.org/docs/latest/branching/) (n.d., docs 1.12.0; DQ-12). Databricks `expect_or_fail` rolls back an update: [Databricks: Manage data quality with pipeline expectations](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ldp/expectations) (2026-10-26; DQ-14; vendor). Basis [S-weaker]: the sources present a pattern, and Netflix says not every table needs coverage. Apply to published datasets only.
- **VAL-05. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Use severity tiers (warn, drop or quarantine, fail). Databricks ([Databricks: Manage data quality with pipeline expectations](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ldp/expectations) (2026-10-26; DQ-14; vendor)) and dbt ([dbt: Add data tests to your DAG](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/data-tests) (2026-09-16; LAKE-19; vendor)) both offer them; Netflix mapped rules to fail or warn ([Scaling Data Quality @ Netflix](https://www.slideshare.net/MichelleFUfford/scaling-data-quality-netflix-76917740) (2017-06-12; DQ-13)). Basis [S].
- **VAL-06. RECOMMENDED. Scope: Lakehouse.** Quarantine bad rows while recording metrics. Databricks shows one flagged table with two filtered views, rather than a separate physical table: [Databricks: Expectation recommendations and advanced patterns](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ldp/expectation-patterns) (2026-10-26; vendor). Basis [S], vendor; other designs are valid.
- **VAL-07. RECOMMENDED. Scope: Streaming sinks.** Use dead-letter topics with context headers enabled: [Kafka Connect Concepts](https://docs.confluent.io/platform/current/connect/concepts.html) (2026-10-16; vendor). Basis [S], vendor. See SMT-03 in the companion document for the silent-failure risk.
- **VAL-08. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: DW.** Unit-test complex transformation logic in development and CI, and not in production. dbt says not to unit-test simple built-ins: [dbt: Unit tests](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/unit-tests) (2026-09-14; vendor). Basis [S], vendor.
- **VAL-09. RECOMMENDED. Scope: Lakehouse, Streaming ingestion.** Validate shape in-process at function boundaries where data has not yet landed, such as with Pandera: [Pandera docs](https://pandera.readthedocs.io/) (n.d.; DQ-06). Basis [S].

## 5. Stage: Transactions

Scope note: dedicated transaction research is in topic file 12, outside this document's brief. This section covers only pipeline-facing and migration-facing transaction boundaries, from the files read.

- **TRN-01. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP to Streaming.** Event publication and business change commit together, through the outbox pattern or log-based CDC. Basis [I]: `data_pipeline_architecture.md` section 11.
- **TRN-02. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse pipelines.** Treat each orchestrated task as a transaction: re-runnable, and never partial. Basis [S]: [Airflow Best Practices](https://airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/best-practices.html) (n.d., Airflow 3.4.0; PIPE-13).
- **TRN-03. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP migrations.** Know whether DDL is transactional. Django says MySQL lacks it, so failed migrations need manual repair: [Django 5.2: Migrations](https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/5.2/topics/migrations/) (n.d., v5.2). Flyway's undo depends on complete prior success: [Flyway: Undo migrations](https://documentation.red-gate.com/fd/undo-migrations-273973334.html) (2024-12-17, updated 2026-05-21; vendor). Basis [S].
- **TRN-04. MANDATORY. Scope: Lakehouse.** Treat transactions as single-table unless the platform confirms otherwise. Basis [S] from the Delta paper ([Delta Lake: High-Performance ACID Table Storage over Cloud Object Stores](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p3411-armbrust.pdf) (2020; LAKE-08)); multi-table support was a private preview in December 2025 and current status was not verified ([Databricks Lakehouse Data Modeling: Myths, Truths, and Best Practices](https://www.databricks.com/blog/databricks-lakehouse-data-modeling-myths-truths-and-best-practices) (2025-12-15; LAKE-06; vendor)).
- **TRN-05. RECOMMENDED. Scope: Lakehouse.** Expect write-rate limits on object-store formats. Basis [S]: [Delta Lake: High-Performance ACID Table Storage over Cloud Object Stores](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p3411-armbrust.pdf) (2020; LAKE-08) reports several transactions per second, a 2020 figure that should be re-checked.
- **TRN-06. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP, PostgreSQL.** `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` cannot run inside a transaction block, so migration tools must be told: [PostgreSQL CREATE INDEX](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-createindex.html) (n.d., PostgreSQL 18.6; PERF-10), [GitLab Migration Style Guide](https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/migration_style_guide.html) (n.d.; OPS-04). Basis [S].

### 6.3 Principles

- **REC-01. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse, any load.** Compare source and target row counts plus at least one aggregate per batch. Basis [I]. No fetched source defines a universal control-record design.
- **REC-02. MANDATORY. Scope: Financial.** Row counts alone do not suffice; amounts must tie. Basis [I], from [Oracle: Account Reconciliation and Subledgers](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/financials/26a/faugl/account-reconciliation-and-subledgers.html) (n.d., release 26A; DQ-16; vendor).
- **REC-03. MANDATORY. Scope: Financial.** Reconcile subledger to GL per period, after periods close to new transactions. Designate control accounts and prevent other sources from posting to them. Classify differences: incomplete accounting, amounts that do not tie to accounting entries, manual journals, and journals from outside the subledgers: [Oracle: Account Reconciliation and Subledgers](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/financials/26a/faugl/account-reconciliation-and-subledgers.html) (n.d., release 26A; DQ-16; vendor). Basis [S], vendor documentation of accounting practice, for Oracle Fusion. **Caveat:** the page calls for identifying and explaining discrepancies, and states no numeric zero-difference target. The notes' proposal of a zero tolerance is a framework decision, and debits equal credits is accounting first principle not sourced here.
- **REC-04. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW.** Attach an audit dimension to fact tables carrying ETL version and quality flags. Kimball treats flags as warnings to users and advises starting simply: [Design Tip #164: Have You Built Your Audit Dimension Yet?](https://www.kimballgroup.com/2014/03/design-tip-164-built-your-audit-dimension/) (2014-03-03; DQ-15). Basis [S].
- **REC-05. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW.** Log screen failures in an error-event schema of event and detail fact tables, kept in the ETL back room: [Kimball Techniques: Error Event Schemas](https://www.kimballgroup.com/?p=19414) (n.d.). Basis [S].
- **REC-06. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP to DW, migrations.** Use row-level diff for migrations and replication validation. reladiff publishes benchmarks of its own, assuming no differences: [erezsh/reladiff](https://github.com/erezsh/reladiff) (n.d.; DQ-17). Basis [S]; the benchmarks are self-reported.
- **REC-07. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP to Streaming.** Reconciliation should include CDC lag and error counts: [Debezium connector for PostgreSQL](https://debezium.io/documentation/reference/stable/connectors/postgresql.html) (n.d., Debezium 3.7; PIPE-06). Basis [S] for metrics.
- **REC-08. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: Lakehouse.** Restate using tagged snapshots rather than in-place overwrite: [Apache Iceberg: Branching and Tagging](https://iceberg.apache.org/docs/latest/branching/) (n.d., docs 1.12.0; DQ-12). Basis [I].

### 7.2 Principles

- **QLT-01. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All.** Select one vocabulary and map others to it. Use the DAMA UK six plus currency as the working vocabulary, and ISO/IEC 25012 as the formal reference. Basis [I]: [GOV.UK: Meet the data quality dimensions](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/meet-the-data-quality-dimensions) (2021-06-24; GOV-04), [DAMA DMBOK Revision](https://dama.org/dama-dmbok-revision/) (n.d.; GOV-02), [ISO/IEC 25012:2008 (IEC webstore)](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/11246) (2008-12-03; GOV-10; scope statement only).
- **QLT-02. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP and DW design.** Map dimensions to enforcement points. Basis [I].

| Dimension | OLTP mechanism | DW or Lakehouse mechanism | Monitoring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Completeness | NOT NULL | Not-null tests | Null-rate trend |
| Uniqueness | PK, UNIQUE | Unique tests, since keys are unenforced | Duplicate count |
| Validity | Types, CHECK, FK to code tables | Accepted-values tests, CHECK where enforced | Invalid-rate trend |
| Consistency | FK, transactions | Relationship tests, reconciliation | Cross-source diff |
| Timeliness and currency | Change timestamps | Freshness thresholds | Freshness and lag |
| Accuracy | Not provable by schema alone; GOV.UK notes valid data is not necessarily accurate | Reconciliation to authoritative sources | Sample audits |

- **QLT-03. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All.** Focus measurement on critical data items, as no single dimension is enough: [GOV.UK: Meet the data quality dimensions](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/meet-the-data-quality-dimensions) (2021-06-24; GOV-04). DMBOK2R explicitly defines critical data elements: [DAMA DMBOK Revision](https://dama.org/dama-dmbok-revision/) (n.d.; GOV-02). Basis [S].
- **QLT-04. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Persist quality metrics over time, because distributional checks need history. Examples: Deequ's metrics repository ([awslabs/deequ](https://github.com/awslabs/deequ) (n.d.; DQ-05)), Databricks' 30-day mean plus or minus 3 standard deviations ([Databricks: Expectation recommendations and advanced patterns](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/ldp/expectation-patterns) (2026-10-26; vendor)), Netflix's null-count distribution ([Scaling Data Quality @ Netflix](https://www.slideshare.net/MichelleFUfford/scaling-data-quality-netflix-76917740) (2017-06-12; DQ-13)), and stored dbt test failures ([dbt: Add data tests to your DAG](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/data-tests) (2026-09-16; LAKE-19; vendor)). Basis [I].
- **QLT-05. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Declare structural assertions alongside the schema, keeping code-based tools for in-process or Spark-native checks. Basis [I].
- **QLT-06. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: All.** Certification tiers for trusted datasets. Airbnb's Midas program is reported this way, but only from a search snippet: [Data Quality at Airbnb](https://medium.com/airbnb-engineering/data-quality-at-airbnb-870d03080469) (n.d.; CASE-39; UNVERIFIED: search snippet only).

### 8.2 Principles

- **GVN-01. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All.** Express schema-level governance as ownership metadata, classification and retention attributes. Standards prescribe accountability and controls, not schema structures. Basis [I], from section 8.1.
- **GVN-02. MANDATORY. Scope: All, with personal or financial data.** Assign accountable owners. DMBOK2R defines the data owner as a business person accountable for decisions within a domain: [DAMA DMBOK Revision](https://dama.org/dama-dmbok-revision/) (n.d.; GOV-02). Basis [I]: DAMA describes DMBOK as not a prescriptive standard ([DAMA Data Management Body of Knowledge](https://dama.org/learning-resources/dama-data-management-body-of-knowledge-dmbok/) (n.d.; GOV-01)), so the definition supplies vocabulary and is not the source of the obligation; MANDATORY is the framework's proposed position.
- **GVN-03. RECOMMENDED. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Grant access to groups, not users, and apply centralized, tag-driven masking or row policies at scale: [Unity Catalog best practices](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/data-governance/unity-catalog/best-practices) (2026-10-30; vendor), [Row filters and column masks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/tables/row-and-column-filters) (2026-11-06; LAKE-04; vendor), [Tag-based masking policies](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/tag-based-masking-policies) (n.d.; vendor), [Overview of Access Control](https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-access-control-overview) (n.d.; vendor). Basis [S], vendor documentation.
- **GVN-04. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse using policy functions.** Match policy parameter types to column types. Databricks shows a type-mismatch example that returned all rows and recommends ANSI mode: [Row filters and column masks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/tables/row-and-column-filters) (2026-11-06; LAKE-04; vendor). ### 9.3 Key type conflict

**Conflict.** dbt's style guide says keys should be strings, and its tutorial hashes natural keys ([How we style our dbt models](https://docs.getdbt.com/best-practices/how-we-style/1-how-we-style-our-dbt-models) (2026-09-10; LAKE-16; vendor), [Building a Kimball dimensional model with dbt](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/kimball-dimensional-model) (2023-04-20; DWM-21; vendor)). Snowflake strongly recommends numeric join keys and advises against hash keys for Data Vault 2.0 clustering ([Snowflake Performance guide](https://www.snowflake.com/en/developers/guides/performance/) (n.d.; LAKE-14; vendor)). Databricks uses identity surrogates for dimensional models, but recommends hash keys for Data Vault ([Loading a Data Warehouse Data Model in Real Time with the Databricks Lakehouse](https://www.databricks.com/blog/2022/11/07/load-edw-dimensional-model-real-time-databricks-lakehouse.html) (2022-11-07; vendor), [Prescriptive Guidance for Implementing a Data Vault Model on the Databricks Lakehouse Platform](https://www.databricks.com/blog/2022/06/24/prescriptive-guidance-for-implementing-a-data-vault-model-on-the-databricks-lakehouse-platform.html) (2022-06-24; vendor)). Context: hash keys allow independent parallel loads, and integers favor join and clustering performance ([Tips for Optimizing the Data Vault Architecture on Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/tips-for-optimizing-the-data-vault-architecture-on-snowflake/) (2020-02-03; vendor)). Treat it as a per-platform decision. All parties are vendors describing their own engines.

### 10.2 Recovery vocabulary

These are NIST's definitions, from locally extracted PDF text: [Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-34r1.pdf) (2010-05, updated 2010-11-11; OPS-17).

- **RTO** "defines the maximum amount of time that a system resource can remain unavailable before there is an unacceptable impact on other system resources, supported mission/business processes, and the MTD."
- **RPO** "represents the point in time, prior to a disruption or system outage, to which mission/business process data can be recovered (given the most recent backup copy of the data) after an outage."
- **MTD** "represents the total amount of time the system owner/authorizing official is willing to accept for a mission/business process outage or disruption and includes all impact considerations."
- "Because the RTO must ensure that the MTD is not exceeded, the RTO must normally be shorter than the MTD."

### 10.3 Backup principles

- **BCK-01. MANDATORY. Scope: All systems of record (OLTP, and any DW or Lakehouse data not reproducible from sources).** A backup is not established until restore has been tested. Microsoft says you do not have a backup strategy until restores succeed in every combination and are checked for consistency: [Back up and restore of SQL Server databases](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/backup-restore/back-up-and-restore-of-sql-server-databases) (2026-08-27; OPS-19; vendor). NIST: "Backup tapes should be tested regularly to ensure that data are being stored correctly and that the files may be retrieved without errors or lost data": [Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-34r1.pdf) (2010-05, updated 2010-11-11; OPS-17). AWS and CISA agree: [AWS: Disaster recovery options in the cloud](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/disaster-recovery-workloads-on-aws/disaster-recovery-options-in-the-cloud.html) (n.d.; OPS-21; vendor), [CISA #StopRansomware Guide](https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide) (2023-09; OPS-22). Basis [S]. This is the most universal rule in the backup evidence.
- **BCK-02. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP.** Physical backup plus continuous log archiving is the basis for point-in-time recovery; logical dumps are a supplement: [PostgreSQL 25.3 Continuous Archiving and PITR](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/continuous-archiving.html) (n.d.; PGMY-11), [MySQL 8.4 Point-in-Time Recovery](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/point-in-time-recovery.html) (n.d.; vendor), [Back up and restore of SQL Server databases](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sql/relational-databases/backup-restore/back-up-and-restore-of-sql-server-databases) (2026-08-27; OPS-19; vendor), [Oracle 19c Backup and Recovery User's Guide: Introduction](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/oracle-database/19/bradv/introduction-backup-recovery.html) (n.d.; OPS-20; vendor). Oracle says logical backups alone are insufficient protection: [Oracle 19c Backup and Recovery User's Guide: Introduction](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/oracle-database/19/bradv/introduction-backup-recovery.html) (n.d.; OPS-20; vendor). Basis [S]. The note authors' inference that RPO is bounded by log-archive frequency is reasonable and is [I].
- **BCK-03. MANDATORY. Scope: OLTP on PostgreSQL.** Test WAL archiving before the first base backup, and make archive commands refuse to overwrite existing files: [PostgreSQL 25.3 Continuous Archiving and PITR](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/continuous-archiving.html) (n.d.; PGMY-11). Basis [S].
- **BCK-04. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP on PostgreSQL.** Use pg_dump for portability across versions and architectures, and not for continuous archiving: [PostgreSQL 25.1 SQL Dump](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/backup-dump.html) (n.d.), [PostgreSQL 25.3 Continuous Archiving and PITR](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/continuous-archiving.html) (n.d.; PGMY-11). - **BCK-05. MANDATORY. Scope: All systems of record.** Replication is not backup. AWS says continuous replication may not protect against corruption or malicious deletion as well as point-in-time backups, and active-active still needs backups: [AWS: Disaster recovery options in the cloud](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/disaster-recovery-workloads-on-aws/disaster-recovery-options-in-the-cloud.html) (n.d.; OPS-21; vendor). - **BCK-06. MANDATORY. Scope: DW, Lakehouse.** Time travel is not backup. Scope: All production systems.** Derive RTO, RPO and MTD from a business impact analysis: [Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-34r1.pdf) (2010-05, updated 2010-11-11; OPS-17). Basis [S], guideline written for federal systems; framework adoption is [I]. ISO 22301:2019 is the business-continuity management standard, known here from a national record because iso.org was blocked: [EVS record of ISO 22301:2019](https://www.evs.ee/en/iso-22301-2019) (valid from 2019-10-30; national record; iso.org returned 403).
- **DR-02. OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL. Scope: All production systems.** Choose a DR tier by RTO, RPO and cost. AWS describes backup and restore, pilot light, warm standby and multi-site active/active: [AWS: Disaster recovery options in the cloud](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/disaster-recovery-workloads-on-aws/disaster-recovery-options-in-the-cloud.html) (n.d.; OPS-21; vendor). NIST describes cold, warm, hot, mobile and mirrored sites: [Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems, NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-34r1.pdf) (2010-05, updated 2010-11-11; OPS-17). RPO and RTO figures by tier were not available in the fetched text, so none are stated.
- **DR-03. MANDATORY. Scope: All production systems.** Rehearse recovery regularly. AWS and Azure both advise periodic testing: [AWS: Disaster recovery options in the cloud](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/disaster-recovery-workloads-on-aws/disaster-recovery-options-in-the-cloud.html) (n.d.; OPS-21; vendor), [Azure SQL: Restore a database from a backup](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-sql/database/recovery-using-backups) (2026-01-26; vendor). Basis [S].
- **DR-04. RECOMMENDED. Scope: OLTP on Azure SQL.** Geo-restore is the most basic option with possible data loss; business-critical applications should use failover groups: [Azure SQL: Restore a database from a backup](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-sql/database/recovery-using-backups) (2026-01-26; vendor). Basis [S], vendor.
- **DR-05. RECOMMENDED. Scope: All.** After recovery, inspect the database, since PostgreSQL advises verifying the desired state: [PostgreSQL 25.3 Continuous Archiving and PITR](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/continuous-archiving.html) (n.d.; PGMY-11). Reconcile derived stores against the restored source ([I]).
- **DR-06. MANDATORY. Scope: Pipelines with CDC.** Include pipeline state in DR planning, because replication slots and connector offsets bind to a database's history. Debezium warns that dropping a slot on the primary loses data: [Debezium connector for PostgreSQL](https://debezium.io/documentation/reference/stable/connectors/postgresql.html) (n.d., Debezium 3.7; PIPE-06). Basis [I] for DR scope. The notes contain no source on re-establishing CDC after a failover.
