# 08 — Data Governance Frameworks and International Standards Relevant to Database Schema Design

Research window: 2026-11-07 (fetches) / written 2026-11-08. Scope: what is formally STANDARDIZED (ISO/IEC, ANSI, OMG, NIST, W3C, DAMA, EDM Council, LF) versus common practice, so a schema-design framework can cite standards by number, part and edition.

Access note that applies throughout: every `www.iso.org` catalogue page returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so ISO-only standards are documented from (a) the IEC webstore (joint ISO/IEC standards share the ISO abstract), (b) national-body mirrors (BSI Knowledge, EVS, DIN Media, Genorma, SCC), (c) the responsible committee's own site (ISO/TC 154 for ISO 8601) or the Maintenance Agency (SIX for ISO 4217, GLEIF for ISO 17442). Where only an abstract/scope was visible, the text says "scope per ISO catalogue". Clause-level content of paywalled standards is NOT paraphrased beyond that. Labels used: **MANDATORY** (legally/contractually required in a named context), **RECOMMENDED** (standards body or framework says to do it; voluntary), **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (reference material; adopt when the context calls for it).

## Q1. DAMA-DMBOK: edition, knowledge areas, Data Modeling & Design, data quality dimensions

### Takeaway
DAMA-DMBOK is a *voluntary body of knowledge*, not a standard: the current text is the "DAMA-DMBOK2 Revised Edition" (2024 maintenance release of the 2nd edition, Technics Publications), organised into 11 knowledge areas with Data Governance at the hub; DMBOK 3.0 is in development. The DAMA UK "Six Primary Dimensions" (accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness, validity) are the most widely re-used DQ dimension set, and the 2024 revision added *currency* as a recognised dimension.

### Cited Findings
- DAMA describes the DMBOK as the "2nd Edition" and states "In 2024 DAMA launched the DAMA DMBOK® 2.0 Revision"; the DAMA-DMBOK® 3.0 project started in 2025 as an "evergreening initiative"; the DMBOK is "the knowledge base for CDMP® certification" but "not a certification or training program itself" — [DAMA® Data Management Body of Knowledge (DAMA-DMBOK®)](https://dama.org/learning-resources/dama-data-management-body-of-knowledge-dmbok/) (page © 2026; n.d. for body text).
- The 2024 revision is "a maintenance release"; "No material changes were made to the overarching data management framework, knowledge areas, or their definitions." Changes: unified terminology; all 12 context diagrams revised; "Interface Developer" renamed Data Integration Developer; Data Owner redefined as a business person accountable for decisions within a domain; "data governance program" renamed "data governance function"; AI governance and ethics added; data described as an intangible asset; Data Quality chapter gained a new appendix, dimension tables refined with examples, "Currency was added as a data quality dimension", critical data elements explicitly defined, ISO content moved from §1.3.5 to an appendix — [DAMA DMBOK Revision](https://dama.org/dama-dmbok-revision/) (n.d., © 2026).
- Revised edition "available on the Technics Publications website by April 2024"; becomes the CDMP exam reference "from October 2024"; it "will replace the previous version as the authoritative resource" — [DAMA-DMBOK®2 REVISED Edition – FAQs](https://dama.org/dama-dmbok2-revised-edition-faqs/) (n.d.).
- DAMA defines data management as "the development, execution, and supervision of plans, policies, programs, and practices" that deliver, control, protect and enhance the value of data; "DAMA-DMBOK® organizes data management into 11 knowledge areas"; six are named as especially critical: Data Governance, Data Quality, Data Security, Data Architecture, Metadata Management, Data Integration & Interoperability — [What is Data Management?](https://dama.org/about-dama/what-is-data-management/) (n.d.).
- The DMBOK2R infographics page lists 14 context diagrams: Data Handling Ethics; Data Governance and Stewardship; Data Architecture; Data Modeling and Design; Data Storage and Operations; Data Security; Data Integration and Interoperability; Document and Content Management; Reference and Master Data; Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence; Metadata Management; Data Quality Management; Big Data and Data Science; Data Management Maturity Assessment. The "Evolved Wheel" is "the DAMA International evolved image of what Data Management looks like and how all these functions interact together"; required citation is "DAMA-DMBOK2R … second edition, revised, Sedona, AZ: Technics Publications, LLC" (image © 2024) — [DAMA-DMBOK® infographics](https://dama.org/dmbok2r-infographics/).
- The full list of the 11 knowledge areas (Data Governance, Data Architecture, Data Modeling and Design, Data Storage and Operations, Data Security, Data Integration and Interoperability, Document and Content Management, Reference and Master Data, Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence, Metadata Management, Data Quality) comes from secondary summaries — [Atlan, "DAMA DMBOK Framework: An Ultimate Guide for 2026"](https://atlan.com/dama-dmbok-framework/) (search-returned, not fetched); Atlan describes Data Modeling and Design as "discovering, analyzing, and representing data requirements as models" and Data Storage and Operations as physical storage design and database operations.
- **CONFLICT (count of knowledge areas):** most DAMA pages say 11; one DAMA article dated January 2026 ("The Seven Deadly Data Sins") was reported by search as saying 10, and one third-party page says 12. Treat 11 as authoritative (DAMA's own "What is Data Management" page) — sources: [dama.org What is Data Management](https://dama.org/about-dama/what-is-data-management/); [search summary of dama.org](https://dama.org/2026/01/04/the-seven-deadly-data-sins/) (not fetched).
- DAMA Dictionary of Data Management: "comprehensive glossary of data management terminology", "over 2,000 terms", "complements the DAMA-DMBOK® by providing clear definitions for terms used in the framework", study aid for CDMP — [DAMA® Dictionary of Data Management](https://dama.org/learning-resources/dama-dictionary-of-data-management/) (n.d.). DAMA's references list gives it as "The DAMA Dictionary of Data Management (2nd ed.) | Technics Publications, LLC | 2011" — [Books Referenced in DMBoK](https://dama.org/books-referenced-in-dmbok/) (search-returned).
- Data Model Scorecard (referenced practice for model quality): Steve Hoberman's book *Data Model Scorecard: Applying the Industry Standard on Data Model Quality* lists ten categories: Correctness, Completeness, Scheme, Structure, Abstraction, Standards, Readability, Definitions, Consistency, Data — [Technics Publications – Data Model Scorecard](https://technicspub.com/data-model-scorecard/) (n.d.; ISBN-13 9781634620826 appears in the cover filename). Hoberman's earlier TDAN columns (2006–2008) used different category wording (e.g., "How consistent is the model with the enterprise?", "How well does the metadata match the data?") — [TDAN, The Data Modeling Addict – July 2006](https://tdan.com/?p=5474) (search-returned).
- DAMA UK whitepaper "The Six Primary Dimensions For Data Quality Assessment" (subtitle "Defining Data Quality Dimensions") is **members-only** on dama-uk.org; the page shows 4 Apr 2025 and the image filename references "DQ_Dimensions_White_Paper2020" — [DAMA UK resource page](https://www.dama-uk.org/resources/the-six-primary-dimensions-for-data-quality-assessment). Academic citations date the original to October 2013 (search summary; e.g., arXiv 2306.15007).
- The six DAMA UK dimensions and definitions, as adopted by the UK Government Data Quality Hub (published 24 June 2021): Accuracy (data reflects real-world facts), Completeness (data needed for a specific use is present; critical vs optional), Uniqueness (records appear only once), Consistency (values agree within a record and across datasets), Timeliness (available when expected and needed), Validity (follows expected format, type, range; "valid data is not necessarily accurate"). Guidance: "No single dimension is sufficient on its own"; measure critical data items regularly; "recommended by the Data Management Association UK (DAMA UK)" while "organisations may define different dimensions" — [GOV.UK, Meet the data quality dimensions](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/meet-the-data-quality-dimensions). IBM's product docs list the same six and attribute them to DAMA — [IBM Docs, Data quality dimensions](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/DSXDOC/wsj/quality/dq-dimensions.html) (search-returned).

### Labels
- Adopting the DMBOK knowledge-area structure: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (DAMA: a body of knowledge, voluntary; basis for CDMP).
- Using the six DAMA UK dimensions (+ currency per DMBOK2R) as the DQ vocabulary in a schema framework: **RECOMMENDED** (DAMA UK's stated purpose was to reduce confusion by giving generic, best-practice definitions; UK government adopted them; conflicts with ISO/IEC 25012's 15 characteristics — see Q4).
- Scoring models with the Data Model Scorecard: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (a practitioner method published by Technics; "industry standard" is the publisher's claim, not a standards body's).

### Inferences
- Because the 2024 revision explicitly left knowledge areas unchanged, framework citations can safely cite "DAMA-DMBOK2 Revised Edition (2024)" for structure and "DMBOK2 (2017)" for the same chapter numbering; only the DQ appendix and terminology differ.
- The DMBOK chapter on Data Modeling & Design is widely reported (secondary sources) to cover conceptual/logical/physical models and multiple modeling schemes (relational, dimensional, object-oriented, fact-based, time-based, NoSQL) and to reference the Data Model Scorecard; this matches the book but was NOT verifiable from any opened dama.org page.

### Gaps
- No opened DAMA page enumerated all 11 knowledge areas or described the Data Modeling & Design chapter contents (modeling schemes, Data Model Scorecard, naming standards). DMBOK is a paid book.
- The 2017 publication year of DMBOK2 could not be confirmed from any opened DAMA page (pages only say "2nd Edition").
- The DAMA UK whitepaper's own text is members-only; definitions were taken from GOV.UK's adoption of it.

## Q2. SQL standard — ISO/IEC 9075 parts, SQL:2023, SQL:2011 temporal tables, SQL:2016 JSON

### Takeaway
ISO/IEC 9075 is a multi-part, paywalled standard maintained by ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32; the current edition is SQL:2023 (Part 1 published 1 June 2023; new Part 16 SQL/PGQ; a native JSON type, feature T801). Temporal tables entered in SQL:2011 Part 2 (application-time periods, system-versioned tables, `WITHOUT OVERLAPS`, `FOR PORTION OF`, `FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF`), and SQL:2016 added the SQL/JSON function set without a JSON type.

### Cited Findings
- ISO/IEC 9075-1:2023 "Information technology. Database languages SQL. Part 1: Framework (SQL/Framework)", published 1 June 2023, status "Current, Under Review"; publisher ISO/IEC; UK committee IST/40; ICS 35.060 — [BSI Knowledge – ISO/IEC 9075-1:2023](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/information-technology-database-languages-sql-part-1-framework-sql-framework). The ISO catalogue entry (search-returned; fetch 403) is [ISO/IEC 9075-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/76583.html) — reported as edition 6, 74 pages, replacing the 2016 edition and its Cor 1:2022; "scope per ISO catalogue": defines the conceptual framework, terms and notation used by the other parts; main change: "addition and refinement of the terms and concepts to support new data types required by incremental parts" (search summary).
- Parts of ISO/IEC 9075 listed for SQL:2023: Framework; Foundation; Call-Level Interface (SQL/CLI); Persistent Stored Modules (SQL/PSM); Management of External Data (SQL/MED); Object Language Bindings (SQL/OLB); Information and Definition Schemas (SQL/Schemata); SQL Routines and Types for the Java Programming Language (SQL/JRT); XML-Related Specifications (SQL/XML); Property Graph Queries (SQL/PGQ) as Part 16. New in SQL:2023: JSON data type (T801), enhanced JSON data type (T802), string-based JSON (T803), SQL/JSON path extensions (T840, T860–T882); SQL/PGQ lets users "query table data as if it were a graph database"; smaller optional features: UNIQUE null treatment (F292), GREATEST/LEAST (T054), underscores in numeric literals (T662). ISO/IEC 9075-16:2023 catalogue: [iso.org/standard/79473.html](https://www.iso.org/standard/79473.html); maintenance portal [standards.iso.org/iso-iec/9075](https://standards.iso.org/iso-iec/9075) — [Wikipedia, SQL:2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQL:2023) (fetched; community reference citing Peter Eisentraut's 4 April 2023 post "SQL:2023 is finished: Here is what's new", whose URL could not be fetched).
- Part numbers: Part 1 Framework, Part 2 Foundation, Part 9 SQL/MED and Part 16 SQL/PGQ were confirmed as normative references of 9075-1:2023 in the search summary ([SCC listing](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/2052731), search-returned). Part 15 (SQL/MDA, multidimensional arrays) is referenced in the brief but was not confirmed from any opened source.
- SQL:2011 temporal features (authors on the ISO SQL committee, IBM): "SQL:2011 was published in December of 2011"; temporal extensions are "part of SQL:2011 Part 2, SQL/Foundation" rather than a new part (a 1995 project for a separate temporal part based on TSQL2 "was cancelled in 2001"). Design choice: no PERIOD data type — "SQL:2011 adds period definitions as metadata to tables", because a new type "would have to also be added to … JDBC, ODBC, and .NET … ETL products, replication solutions". Periods are closed-open; a period definition implies a constraint that end > start. Valid time → application-time period tables (user-named period, `PERIOD FOR EPeriod (EStart, EEnd)`); transaction time → system-versioned tables (period must be named `SYSTEM_TIME`, columns `GENERATED ALWAYS AS ROW START/END`, table clause `WITH SYSTEM VERSIONING`); "at most one application-time period and at most one system-time period per table". Constraints: `PRIMARY KEY (ENo, EPeriod WITHOUT OVERLAPS)`; temporal referential constraints. DML: `UPDATE … FOR PORTION OF EPeriod FROM DATE '2011-02-03' TO DATE '2011-09-10'` and the same for DELETE (row-splitting fires INSERT triggers for the new rows). Queries on system-versioned tables: `FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF`, `FROM … TO` (closed-open), `BETWEEN … AND` (closed-closed); default is `AS OF CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` (current rows only). Users cannot set system-period columns; INSERT sets start = transaction timestamp and end = max of the type; history "cannot be tampered with, which is critical to meet auditing and compliance regulations". Column types may be DATE or a timestamp type (both the same); "most implementations will provide the TIMESTAMP type with the highest fractional seconds precision". Bitemporal tables combine both (the standard itself does not use the term) — [Kulkarni & Michels, "Temporal features in SQL:2011", SIGMOD Record 41(3), September 2012, pp. 34–43](https://sigmodrecord.org/publications/sigmodRecord/1209/pdfs/07.industry.kulkarni.pdf).
- SQL:2016 (ISO/IEC 9075:2016, released December 2016) added, in Part 2 SQL/Foundation: JSON constructor functions (JSON_OBJECT, JSON_ARRAY and aggregates), query functions (JSON_EXISTS, JSON_VALUE, JSON_QUERY, JSON_TABLE), the IS JSON predicate and the SQL/JSON path language; row pattern recognition (MATCH_RECOGNIZE); polymorphic table functions; LISTAGG; DECFLOAT; datetime formatting templates. "The standard has no native JSON type and stores JSON as strings"; "ISO/IEC 9075-2:2023 later introduced a JSON type (feature T801)". Companion technical reports ISO/IEC TR 19075-5/-6/-7 describe row pattern recognition, JSON and PTFs — [Markus Winand, modern-sql.com, "What's New in SQL:2016" (15 June 2017)](https://modern-sql.com/blog/2017-06/whats-new-in-sql-2016).
- Committee: ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32 "Data management and interchange" is named as the technical committee on the IEC webstore entry for ISO/IEC 11179-5 ([IEC webstore 21986](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/21986)); the SC 32 committee page [iso.org/committee/45342.html](https://www.iso.org/committee/45342.html) itself returned 403 (URL from brief; NOT verified).

### Labels
- Conforming to ISO/IEC 9075 syntax where a feature is standardized (e.g., `PERIOD FOR`, `WITH SYSTEM VERSIONING`, SQL/JSON functions): **RECOMMENDED** (portability rationale from the standard's authors — vendor-neutral syntax; note Kulkarni & Michels observe commercial adoption was "rather slow" and that only one implementation existed in 2012, so vendor dialects still dominate).
- Modelling valid-time with a pair of datetime columns + period metadata rather than a custom period type: **RECOMMENDED** (explicit SQL:2011 design rationale about ecosystem compatibility).
- Using SQL/PGQ or the SQL:2023 JSON type: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (optional features; Eisentraut flagged PGQ as a "future" item per secondary summary).

### Inferences
- A schema framework can cite "ISO/IEC 9075-2:2023, feature T801 (JSON data type)" for native JSON columns and "ISO/IEC 9075-2 (SQL:2011 onward), application-time period tables / system-versioned tables" for temporal design; feature IDs are the precise way to cite optional SQL features.
- Because SQL:2016 JSON was function-based over strings, databases that predate 2023 differ on JSON storage; schema frameworks should treat JSON column typing as vendor-specific.

### Gaps
- Could not open any ISO catalogue page for 9075 parts; the full current part list with 2023 edition status per part (and the status of Parts 3, 10, 13, 15) is unverified from primary sources.
- Eisentraut's blog post URL (the most-cited primary summary) returned 404 at the URL tried.

## Q3. Metadata and naming — ISO/IEC 11179 (Parts 3 and 5), ISO/IEC 19763, ISO/IEC 21838

### Takeaway
ISO/IEC 11179 is the only international standard that directly governs *naming and registering data elements*: Part 5 (2015, 3rd ed.; 4th ed. at DIS stage) gives naming principles and rules for naming conventions for concepts, data element concepts, conceptual domains, data elements and value domains; Part 3 (2023) is the registry metamodel. The "object class term + property term + representation term (+ qualifier)" convention is the practice associated with 11179-5 but could only be confirmed from secondary sources.

### Cited Findings
- ISO/IEC 11179-5:2015 "Information technology – Metadata registries (MDR) – Part 5: Naming principles", Edition 3.0, published 2015-03-18, 25 pages, TC ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 32, ICS 35.040.50. Scope per ISO catalogue: provides instruction for naming "concept, data element concept, conceptual domain, data element, and value domain" as defined in ISO/IEC 11179-3; "describes naming in metadata registries, sets out principles and rules for developing naming conventions, and provides example conventions". The abstract does not itself mention object class/property/representation term — [IEC webstore – ISO/IEC 11179-5:2015](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/21986) (webstore displayed "CHF 0.-", likely a display artefact; treat as paywalled).
- A 4th edition is in progress: [ISO/IEC DIS 11179-5](https://www.iso.org/standard/92786.html) (search-returned; "Draft International Standard … will replace ISO/IEC 11179-5:2015"). The 1995 first edition was titled "Naming and identification principles for data elements" (withdrawn) — [SCC record](https://ccn-scc.ca/standardsdb/standards/8100171) (search-returned).
- ISO/IEC 11179-3:2023 "Metamodel for registry common facilities" (January 2023, 97 pages) replaced 11179-3:2013 ("Registry metamodel and basic attributes"); clause structure reported: Clause 6 Core Model (generic "registry item"), 7 identification, 8 designation and definition, 9 registration, 10 classification, 11 mapping; extension parts 31 (data specification registration), 32 (concept systems), 33 (data sets), 35 (model registration); Amd 1:2026 "Enhancements to Item Mapping" listed — [ISO/IEC 11179-3:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78915.html) (search-returned; details from [SCC](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/8184414)/[Genorma](https://genorma.com/en/standards/iso-iec-11179-3-2023) search summaries; not fetched).
- Community summary of the 11179 model: a data element concept is a concept combined with an object class ("income" + "person" → "net income of person"); a data element is one of possibly several representations of a data element concept; a value domain is "the permitted range of values for a characteristic of an object class" (e.g., sex of person: M, F, U); registries using it include NCI caDSR, AIHW METeOR, USHIK, Aristotle Metadata Registry — [Wikipedia, ISO/IEC 11179](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_11179) (fetched; lists parts 1–7 with years 2015/2005/2013/2004/2015/2015/2019 — now partly outdated for Part 3).
- DDI Alliance mapping to 11179: object class ↔ DDI Universe; property ↔ DDI Concept; representation ↔ the Variable's representation; data element concept ↔ ConceptualVariable; data element ↔ RepresentedVariable; "A Variable without a concept reference is not compliant with ISO/IEC 11179"; identification by Agency + ID + Version; Name/Label/Description on primary objects "following 11179-5"; ISO/IEC 11179-1 first edition dated 1999-12-01 — [DDI Lifecycle Technical Guide – ISO/IEC 11179](https://ddi-lifecycle-technical-guide.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Other%20Standards/ISO11179.html) (n.d.).
- Representation term ("class word"): "a word or combination of words that semantically represents a data element's data type (value domain)", e.g., "ID" in PersonID; common terms Amount, Code, Date, DateTime, Identifier, Indicator, Measure, Name, Text, Year; defined in ISO/IEC 11179 and the UN/CEFACT Core Components Technical Specification; used by NIEM, GJXDM, ebXML; the page notes that 11179 does not require representation terms to be drawn from a fixed "representation class" list — [Wikipedia, Representation term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_term) (fetched).
- Three-part naming convention (object class term + property term + representation term, optional qualifier term; example "EmployeeAddressText", "EmployeeMailingAddressText") — reported in a Missouri state government naming standard ([CC-DMDataElementNamingStandards, 2005](https://oa.mo.gov/sites/default/files/CC-DMDataElementNamingStandards01122005.pdf), search-returned; fetch 403). **Secondary only.**
- **CONFLICT (usefulness of 11179 names):** an xml-dev list contributor called the convention responsible for "some of the ugliest and most unusable names in xml" (search summary of [lists.xml.org thread](https://lists.xml.org/archive/lists/xml-dev/months/200506/messages/214), not fetched) versus government registries (caDSR, METeOR, USHIK) that mandate it internally.
- ISO/IEC 19763 "Metamodel framework for interoperability (MFI)": Part 1 Framework gives the series' purpose, concepts, architecture and requirements; other parts are normative UML metamodels for registering models (ontologies, process models, service models, role/goal models, information models, form designs); the 2015 edition of Part 1 is withdrawn and a 2023 edition exists — [ISO/IEC 19763-1:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/64637.html) (search-returned; not fetched).
- ISO/IEC 21838-2:2021 "Top-level ontologies (TLO) – Basic Formal Ontology (BFO)", published 31 December 2021 (BSI edition), status Current; scope: definitions of BFO-2020 terms and relations, OWL 2 and CL axiomatizations, documentation of conformity to "the requirements specified for top-level ontologies in ISO/IEC 21838-1"; excludes ontology languages and reasoners — [BSI Knowledge – ISO/IEC 21838-2:2021](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/information-technology-top-level-ontologies-tlo-basic-formal-ontology-bfo). Part 1 (requirements, 2021) and Part 3 (DOLCE) exist per search; part numbering for TUpper disagreed between sources (Part 3 vs 4).

### Labels
- Naming data elements with a documented convention and registering them with definitions (11179-4/-5 intent): **RECOMMENDED** (ISO/IEC 11179-5 scope: "principles and rules for developing naming conventions"; required only where an organisation/regulator mandates a registry, e.g., health statistics registries).
- Adopting the specific object-class/property/representation-term form: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (not visible in the official abstract; secondary sources show it; readability objections exist).
- ISO/IEC 19763 and 21838: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (model/ontology registration and top-level ontologies — relevant to semantic layers, not to physical schema).

### Inferences
- For a schema framework, 11179-5 is best cited as the authority for *having* a naming convention (consistent terms, one concept per name, representation/class words), while the concrete column-naming pattern should be presented as a convention "aligned with ISO/IEC 11179-5" rather than "required by" it.

### Gaps
- Could not open 11179-5 text; the exact normative rules (e.g., semantic/syntactic/lexical/uniqueness rules) are not confirmed beyond the scope statement.
- The official SC 32 WG 2 site (metadata-standards.org) returned 404 at the path tried.

## Q4. Data quality standards — ISO 8000 (61, 110), ISO/IEC 25012, ISO/IEC 25024, DAMA UK dimensions

### Takeaway
Two distinct standards families define data quality: ISO/IEC 25012:2008 (SQuaRE data quality *model*, 15 characteristics split inherent/system-dependent, from JTC 1/SC 7) with ISO/IEC 25024:2015 (*measures*), and ISO 8000 (TC 184/SC 4) which standardizes data-quality *management processes* (8000-61) and *master-data exchange* requirements (8000-110). They overlap only loosely with the DAMA UK six dimensions; a framework should pick one vocabulary and map the others.

### Cited Findings
- ISO/IEC 25012:2008 "Software engineering – Software product Quality Requirements and Evaluation (SQuaRE) – Data quality model", Edition 1.0, 2008-12-03, 13 pages, CHF 100, TC ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 7. Scope per ISO catalogue: "defines a general data quality model for data retained in a structured format within a computer system"; usable to set DQ requirements, define measures, plan/perform evaluations (incl. legislative compliance); quality attributes "categorized into fifteen characteristics" viewed from "inherent and system dependent" points of view — [IEC webstore – ISO/IEC 25012:2008](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/11246).
- Inherent quality "comes from the data itself, such as its domain values, relationships between values, and metadata"; system-dependent quality "is reached and preserved within a computer system". Verified definitions: Accuracy — "degree to which data has attributes that correctly represent the true value of the intended attribute" (syntactic and semantic); Completeness — "degree to which data has values for all expected attributes and related entity instances"; Consistency — "degree to which data is free from contradiction and coherent with other data" — [iso25000.com – ISO/IEC 25012](https://iso25000.com/index.php/en/iso-25000-standards/iso-25012) (© 2024). Additional characteristics named (no full mapping): Credibility, Currentness, Accessibility (iso25000.com); Availability, Confidentiality, Compliance, Traceability (arc42) — [arc42 quality model – ISO/IEC 25012](https://quality.arc42.org/standards/iso-iec-25012) (CC BY-SA, Gernot Starke). The brief's full list of 15 (accuracy, completeness, consistency, credibility, currentness, accessibility, compliance, confidentiality, efficiency, precision, traceability, understandability, availability, portability, recoverability) is consistent with these partial lists but was not fully verified from an opened source.
- ISO/IEC 25024:2015 "Systems and software engineering – SQuaRE – Measurement of data quality", Edition 1.0, 2015-10-20, 45 pages, CHF 181. Scope per ISO catalogue: data quality measures for the 25012 characteristics; "basic set of measures for each characteristic", target entities across the data life cycle, guidance for applying and for defining own measures; applies to structured data in "legacy systems, data warehouses, and the web"; "does not define rating ranges or grades"; excludes knowledge representation, data mining and statistical significance of samples — [IEC webstore – ISO/IEC 25024:2015](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/23610). ISO reports it "last reviewed and confirmed in 2022" — [ISO/IEC 25024:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/35749.html) (search-returned).
- ISO 8000-61:2016-11 "Data quality – Part 61: Data quality management: Process reference model", 21 pages, original language English, status Current, ICS 25.040.40, EUR 154.60 (PDF) — [DIN Media – ISO 8000-61](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/standard/iso-8000-61/266302693). Scope per ISO catalogue (search summary): "specifies the processes required for data quality management. Each process is defined by a purpose, outcomes and activities"; covers fundamental principles, process structure, lower-level processes, "the relationship between data quality management and data governance", implementation requirements; used "to assess process capability or organizational maturity"; applies to structured data and to less structured data (images, audio, documents). Table-of-contents structure reported: Implementation processes (planning, control, assurance, improvement), Data-Related Support processes (architecture, transfer, operations, security), Resource Provision processes (organization, human resources) — [iteh listing](https://iteh.es/catalog/standards/iso/0b5737db-6738-48ff-8c0e-0c083adf7194/iso-8000-61-2016) (search-returned; DNS failure on fetch).
- ISO 8000-110:2021 "Data quality – Part 110: Master data: Exchange of characteristic data: Syntax, semantic encoding, and conformance to data specification", Edition 2 (replaces 2009), ISO/TC 184/SC 4, CHF 135. Scope per ISO catalogue (search summary): "requirements for the exchange of messages that contain master data consisting of characteristic data … can be checked by computer"; requirements on access to data dictionaries for decoding; excludes accuracy, provenance and completeness (accuracy is ISO 8000-130); "necessary but not sufficient" for master-data quality; cites ISO 9735, ISO 13584, ISO 15926, ISO 22745 as exchange formats — [ISO 8000-110:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/78501.html) (search-returned; not fetched).
- DAMA UK six dimensions: see Q1 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/meet-the-data-quality-dimensions)); DMBOK2R added "currency" ([DAMA revision page](https://dama.org/dama-dmbok-revision/)).
- **CONFLICT (dimension vocabularies):** ISO/IEC 25012 has 15 characteristics including system-dependent ones (availability, portability, recoverability) that DAMA UK does not treat as data-quality dimensions; DAMA UK's "uniqueness" and "timeliness" have no identically named 25012 characteristic (closest: consistency/currentness). GOV.UK explicitly allows that "organisations may define different dimensions".

### Labels
- Defining DQ requirements against a named model (25012) and measuring with 25024-style measures: **RECOMMENDED** (ISO abstract: usable "to establish data quality requirements, define data quality measures, or plan and perform data quality evaluations"); **MANDATORY** only where a regulator or contract requires it (none found).
- Running a DQ management process per ISO 8000-61: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (reference model for capability/maturity assessment; certification bodies use it — search summary).
- Conforming master-data messages to ISO 8000-110: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (industrial supply-chain contexts; "necessary but not sufficient").

### Inferences
- For *schema* design the actionable 25012 characteristics are the inherent ones (accuracy, completeness, consistency, credibility, currentness) — they map to constraints (NOT NULL, CHECK, FK), temporal columns and provenance columns; system-dependent ones (availability, recoverability, portability) map to platform/ops controls.

### Gaps
- Full 15-item list with inherent/system-dependent/both mapping not verified from an opened source (standard is paywalled; secondary pages showed only images).
- ISO 8000 series inventory (e.g., 8000-1, -2, -8, -62 maturity model, -100 series) not verified; the UALR ICIQ 2017 paper page on 8000-61 redirected to a department page.

## Q5. Governance standards — ISO/IEC 38505-1, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, ISO 15489-1, EDM Council DCAM, DGI framework, COBIT 2019

### Takeaway
Governance standards operate above the schema: ISO/IEC 38505-1 (data governance for governing bodies; 2017 edition withdrawn 20 Aug 2026 and replaced by a 2026 edition), ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (ISMS requirements; Clauses 4–10 non-excludable; Annex A controls selected by risk), ISO 15489-1:2016 (records concepts/principles incl. metadata for records), plus two proprietary/industry frameworks (DCAM v3, members-only; DGI's 10-component framework, free) and COBIT 2019's APO14 "Managed Data" objective.

Practice-level titles (APO14.01–.11) not verified.

- ISO/IEC 27001 Clauses 4–10: **MANDATORY** *when* an organisation claims conformity/certification (ISO abstract wording); Annex A controls: **RECOMMENDED/risk-selected** (vendor summaries; not from the abstract).

## Q6. Reference-data standards schemas should use — ISO 3166, ISO 4217, ISO 8601, ISO 639, ISO 20022, ISO 17442 (LEI), GS1

### Takeaway
ISO's country, currency and language code lists (ISO 3166, 4217, 639) are explicitly free to use and are maintained by Maintenance Agencies (SIX for 4217); ISO 8601-1:2019 governs date/time string representation; ISO 20022 supplies a free financial Data Dictionary/Business Process Catalogue; ISO 17442 LEIs are the only reference identifiers here that regulators have made mandatory in named contexts.

### Cited Findings
- ISO 3166: Part 1 country codes (alpha-2 "recommended for general use", alpha-3, numeric-3), Part 2 subdivision codes (country alpha-2 + separator + up to three alphanumerics, e.g., ID-RI), Part 3 formerly used names; current editions are the 2020 editions of Parts 1–3 (2013 and earlier withdrawn); names come from UN sources; maintained by the ISO 3166 Maintenance Agency; codes available on the Online Browsing Platform; "ISO allows free-of-charge use of its country, currency and language codes"; first published as ISO 3166:1974 — [ISO 3166 — Country Codes](https://www.iso.org/iso-3166-country-codes.html) and [ISO 3166-1:2020](https://www.iso.org/standard/72482.html) (both search-returned; fetch 403). Numeric-3 codes originate in UN M.49 (Wikipedia, search-returned).
- ISO 4217: SIX is "the official Maintenance Agency of these currency codes under ISO 4217" (Secretariat, on behalf of ISO and SNV), publishes the lists "free of charge": List One (current currencies & funds; XLS/XML), List Two (current fund codes; DOC), List Three (historical; XLS/XML). Fields: Entity, Currency, Alphabetic Code, Numeric Code, **Minor Unit**. Lists change when a currency is created, withdrawn or altered. Example amendment effective 1 January 2026: Bulgaria adopts EUR (978, minor unit 2); BGN (975, minor unit 2) moves to List Three; fixed rate EUR 1 = BGN 1.95583; dual price display 08.08.2025–08.08.2026 — [SIX – Data Standards (ISO 4217)](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/financial-information/data-standards.html) (amendment ~180 per filename).
- ISO 8601-1:2019 "Date and time — Representations for information interchange — Part 1: Basic rules", published 2019-03-25, ISO stage 90.92 (under revision), ISO/TC 154 WG5, ICS 01.140.30, ISO store ID 70907. Scope: representations of Gregorian calendar dates and 24-hour clock times and composite elements as character strings, including "times and time shifts based on Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)"; excludes non-Gregorian calendars and non-24-hour clocks — [ISO/TC 154 – ISO 8601-1:2019](https://isotc154.org/standards/iso-8601-1-2019). Amendment 1:2022 and Part 2 "Extensions" (ISO 8601-2:2019) reported by search ([iteh listing](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/b42c90ed-008a-40be-b79a-27e15c0de2dc/iso-8601-1-2019), search-returned; page body failed to load). The 8601-1 scope statement "does not address character encoding" (search summary).
- ISO 639:2023 "Code for individual languages and language groups" consolidates the earlier parts into Sets: Set 1 (two-letter; formerly 639-1), Set 2 (three-letter; 639-2), Set 3 (three-letter, all individual languages incl. extinct; 639-3), Set 5 (three-letter language groups; 639-5); maintenance delegated to Infoterm (Set 1), Library of Congress (Sets 2, 5), SIL International (Set 3); published Nov 2023 — [ISO 639 — Language code](https://www.iso.org/iso-639-language-code) and [SCC – ISO 639:2023](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/8184254) (both search-returned; not fetched).
- ISO 20022 (UNIversal Financial Industry message scheme): the Repository "consists of two major parts: the Data Dictionary and the Business Process Catalogue"; Dictionary Items are "Business Concepts, Data Types and Message Concepts"; the repository offers "free access to a Data Dictionary of business and message components and a Business Process Catalogue containing message models and corresponding XML and/or ASN.1 schemas"; a 2025 document covers generation of JSON Schema (Draft 2020-12) from ISO 20022 — [ISO 20022 Repository](https://www.iso20022.org/financial-repository), [FAQ](https://www.iso20022.org/faq), [JSON Schema generation paper (10 June 2025)](https://www.iso20022.org/sites/default/files/media/file/ISO_20022_Generation_of_JSON_Schema_Draft_2020_12_for_ISO_20022_2013_10June2025.pdf) (all search-returned; iso20022.org returned 403/404 to fetch). Standard parts: [ISO 20022-1:2004](https://www.iso.org/standard/40337.html) (search-returned) is reported withdrawn and replaced by ISO 20022-1:2026, with the series reported as nine parts (April 2026) vs an older FAQ saying eight — **CONFLICT/unverified**.
- ISO 17442 / LEI: the LEI is "a unique 20-character alphanumeric code" (example 506700GE1G29325QX363); the LEI follows four ISO 17442 principles (global standard, one identifier per legal entity, backed by high-quality data, public good free of charge); timeline: ISO 17442 Part 1 published 2012, Part 2 (LEI in X.509 digital certificates) 2019; GLEIF established 2014 under Swiss law, overseen by the Regulatory Oversight Committee, backed by G20/FSB. Regulatory mandates: Dodd-Frank (2010) origin; CFTC rule (2013) required the LEI; EMIR (2012) and MiFID II/MiFIR (2018) implementations; RBI (2021) cross-border transactions; Korea FSC (2023); Japan FSA (2024) OTC derivatives — [GLEIF – ISO 17442: The LEI Code Structure](https://www.gleif.org/en/about-lei/iso-17442-the-lei-code-structure). (The current catalogue edition ISO 17442-1:2020 named in the brief is not stated on the GLEIF page.)
- GS1 identification keys: page returned 403; not verified (gap).

### Labels
- Storing country/currency/language as ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 (or alpha-3)/ISO 4217 alpha codes/ISO 639 identifiers: **RECOMMENDED** (ISO: alpha-2 "recommended for general use"; code lists free; Maintenance Agencies publish machine-readable lists). Choice of alpha-2 vs alpha-3 vs numeric: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (ISO offers all three for different purposes).
- Using ISO 4217 *Minor Unit* to set decimal scale per currency: **RECOMMENDED** (SIX publishes Minor Unit as a field of List One; minor units change over time via amendments, so scale must be data-driven, not hard-coded).
- Exchanging date/time as ISO 8601-1 strings: **RECOMMENDED** for interchange (scope is "representations … for information interchange", not storage types).
- LEI (ISO 17442) for legal-entity identification: **MANDATORY** in the regulated reporting contexts GLEIF lists (CFTC 2013, MiFID II 2018, RBI 2021, Korea 2023, Japan 2024); **RECOMMENDED** elsewhere.
- ISO 20022 dictionary alignment: **MANDATORY** only where a payment scheme mandates ISO 20022 messaging (not verified here); otherwise **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** as a free business-concept vocabulary.

### Inferences
- Reference-code columns should be modelled as FKs to maintained code tables loaded from the Maintenance Agency lists (SIX XML; ISO OBP) with effective-dating, because codes are amended (Bulgaria EUR 2026 example) and historical codes move to "List Three"/ISO 3166-3.

### Gaps
- iso.org pages for 3166/4217/8601/639 and iso20022.org could not be fetched; content relies on search snippets and Maintenance Agency/TC sites.
- ISO 4217:2015 catalogue edition and ISO 8601-2:2019 scope not verified from an opened page.
- GS1 General Specifications not accessible (403).

## Q7. Modeling standards — ANSI/SPARC, Chen ER, IDEF1X (FIPS 184), UML (ISO/IEC 19505), OMG CWM, W3C DCAT, OpenLineage

### Takeaway
Of the modeling references only four are formal standards: FIPS 184 (IDEF1X, NIST 1993, since withdrawn), ISO/IEC 19505 (UML 2 infrastructure, 2012, confirmed 2025), OMG CWM 1.1 (2003, formal) and W3C DCAT 3 (Recommendation, 22 Aug 2024). ANSI/SPARC (1975/1978) and Chen (1976) are foundational literature, and OpenLineage is an open-source LF specification (JSON Schema, Apache-2.0) — widely used but not a de-jure standard.

### Cited Findings
- ANSI/X3/SPARC: Tsichritzis & Klug, "The ANSI/X3/SPARC DBMS framework report of the study group on database management systems", *Information Systems* 3(3):173–191, January 1978, Elsevier, DOI 10.1016/0306-4379(78)90001-7 — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016%2F0306-4379%2878%2990001-7). The 1975 Interim Report (FDT – Bulletin of ACM SIGMOD 7(2):1–140) introduced the external/conceptual/internal three-schema architecture and is described as never having become a formal standard — [Wikipedia, ANSI-SPARC Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI-SPARC_Architecture) (search-returned; the DTIC copy returned 403/hang-up).
- Chen, Peter P. S., "The entity-relationship model: toward a unified view of data", 1976; MIT Sloan Working Paper 839-76 — [MIT DSpace record](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/47638); journal version ACM TODS 1(1):9–36, DOI 10.1145/320434.320440 (ACM page 403; DOI via bibliographic search); scanned journal copy at [NUS mirror](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~lingtw/papers/tods76.chen.pdf) (fetched as image-only PDF, 28 pages). Abstract (search snippet): a data model that "incorporates some of the important semantic information about the real world" plus a diagramming technique; shows how network, relational and entity-set models derive from it; earlier version presented at VLDB 1975.
- FIPS PUB 184 "Integration Definition for Information Modeling (IDEF1X)", Category: Software Standard, Subcategory: Modeling Techniques; "Issued December 21, 1993" (effective 30 June 1994 per search); 161 pages; Approving Authority: Secretary of Commerce; Maintenance Agency: NIST Computer Systems Laboratory; based on ICAM IISS Vol. V Part 4 "IDEF1 Extended" (Nov 1985). Abstract: "describes the IDEF1X modeling language (semantics and syntax) and associated rules and techniques, for developing a logical model of data … permits the construction of semantic data models which may serve to support the management of data as a resource, the integration of information systems, and the building of computer databases." Objectives include "an application-independent view of data which can be validated by users and transformed into a physical database design". §12 Waivers: agency heads may waive compliance only when it "would adversely affect the accomplishment of the mission" or "cause a major adverse financial impact", with Federal Register publication — [NIST – FIPS PUB 184 (PDF)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/FIPS/fipspub184.pdf). Withdrawn 2 September 2008; successor ISO/IEC/IEEE 31320-2:2012 — [Wikipedia, IDEF1X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDEF1X) (search-returned).
- ISO/IEC 19505-1:2012 "Information technology – Object Management Group Unified Modeling Language (OMG UML) – Part 1: Infrastructure", Edition 1.0, 2012-04-20, 220 pages, CHF 227, ISO/IEC JTC 1. Abstract: establishes "revision 2 of the Unified Modeling Language (UML)" for system architects, software engineers and developers, also for "modeling business and similar processes" — [IEC webstore – ISO/IEC 19505-1:2012](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/10689). ISO reports it reviewed and confirmed 2025 — [ISO/IEC 19505-1:2012](https://www.iso.org/standard/32624.html) (search-returned).
- OMG Common Warehouse Metamodel (CWM™) version 1.1, March 2003, formal/03-03-02; uses XMI to interchange data-warehouse metadata based on a MOF-specified metamodel; modules cover relational, multidimensional, OLAP, transformation and data mining; the page does not mention ISO adoption — [OMG – CWM](https://www.omg.org/spec/CWM/).
- W3C DCAT 3 "Data Catalog Vocabulary (DCAT) – Version 3", W3C Recommendation 22 August 2024 (DCAT 2 was 4 February 2020); RDF vocabulary for interoperable data catalogs; classes Catalog (sub-class of Dataset), Dataset, Distribution, DataService, DatasetSeries (new in v3); properties incl. dcat:dataset, dcat:distribution, dcat:accessURL, dcat:downloadURL, dcat:byteSize, dcat:mediaType, spdx:checksum, dcat:endpointURL, dcat:servesDataset, versioning (dcat:version, dcat:previousVersion, dcat:hasCurrentVersion) — [W3C – DCAT 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/).
- OpenLineage: "An open framework for data lineage collection and analysis"; spec "formalized as a JSON Schema" (OpenLineage.json) with an OpenAPI spec (OpenLineage.yml); entities RunEvent, Run (UUID; UUIDv7 recommended), Job (name within namespace), Dataset (unique name within a datasource namespace, e.g., db.host.database.schema.table); a run requires a START and a COMPLETE/FAIL/ABORT event; event types RunEvent, JobEvent, DatasetEvent; facets extend entities (job: sourceCodeLocation, sourceCode; run: nominalTime, parent, errorMessage, sql; dataset: schema, dataSource, lifecycleStateChange, version; input: dataQualityMetrics, dataQualityAssertions; output: outputStatistics); facet `_schemaURL` must be immutable/canonical; custom facets need a project prefix; Apache-2.0; current docs version 1.55.0; governed by a Technical Steering Committee, Linux Foundation footer — [OpenLineage – Object Model](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/object-model), [OpenLineage spec (GitHub)](https://github.com/OpenLineage/OpenLineage/blob/main/spec/OpenLineage.md), [openlineage.io](https://openlineage.io/), [LF AI & Data project page](https://lfaidata.foundation/projects/openlineage/) (last updated 2024-06-18; body not rendered).

### Labels
- Three-schema separation (external/conceptual/internal) and conceptual→logical→physical progression: **RECOMMENDED** (ANSI/SPARC; FIPS 184 objective (d) "application-independent view … transformed into a physical database design"); never a de-jure standard.
- IDEF1X notation: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (was **MANDATORY** for US federal agencies 1994–2008 under FIPS 184 with waivers; now withdrawn).
- UML class diagrams for data models: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (ISO/IEC 19505 standardizes the language, not its use for schemas).
- DCAT 3 for catalog metadata: **RECOMMENDED** for publishing dataset metadata on the web (W3C Recommendation status); OpenLineage facets for lineage: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (open-source spec, not a standards-body standard).
- CWM: **OPTIONAL/CONTEXTUAL** (2003, no update; legacy tool interchange).

### Inferences
- OpenLineage's dataset naming (namespace + db.host.database.schema.table) and `schema` facet are the de-facto interchange shape for physical schema metadata between tools; DCAT is the de-jure one for dataset-level metadata. Frameworks should cite W3C DCAT 3 (2024) as the standard and OpenLineage as the operational convention.

### Gaps
- The 1975 ANSI/SPARC interim report text was not opened; three-schema details are from secondary sources.
- Whether CWM was ever adopted as an ISO standard could not be confirmed (OMG page silent).

## Q8. Mandatory vs reference; cost and paywalls

### Takeaway
Almost everything in this set is voluntary reference material; the only items with a legal/regulatory "must" in named contexts are the LEI (ISO 17442, via CFTC/ESMA/RBI/FSC/FSA mandates), ISO/IEC 27001 Clauses 4–10 (when conformity is claimed), and historically FIPS 184 (US federal, 1994–2008). ISO code *lists* (3166, 4217, 639) are free; the standards documents themselves are paywalled (CHF 100–227 per part observed).

### Cited Findings
- Free: ISO 3166/4217/639 code lists ("ISO allows free-of-charge use of its country, currency and language codes" — [iso.org ISO 3166 page](https://www.iso.org/iso-3166-country-codes.html), search-returned); SIX ISO 4217 lists "free of charge" ([SIX](https://www.six-group.com/en/products-services/financial-information/data-standards.html)); ISO 20022 repository "free access" ([FAQ](https://www.iso20022.org/faq), search-returned); FIPS 184 PDF at NIST; W3C DCAT; OMG CWM; OpenLineage (Apache-2.0); DGI framework pages; GOV.UK; LEI data is "a public good available free of charge" ([GLEIF](https://www.gleif.org/en/about-lei/iso-17442-the-lei-code-structure)).
- Paywalled (observed prices): ISO/IEC 25012:2008 CHF 100 ([IEC](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/11246)); ISO/IEC 25024:2015 CHF 181 ([IEC](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/23610)); ISO/IEC 27001:2022 CHF 155 ([IEC](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/79694)); ISO/IEC 19505-1:2012 CHF 227 ([IEC](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/10689)); ISO 8000-61:2016 EUR 154.60 ([DIN Media](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/standard/iso-8000-61/266302693)); ISO 15489-1:2016 EUR 206.76 ([EVS](https://www.evs.ee/en/iso-15489-1-2016)); ISO 8000-110:2021 CHF 135 (search); ISO/IEC 9075 parts sold per part (search). DMBOK is a purchased book (Technics Publications); DAMA UK whitepaper is members-only ([DAMA UK](https://www.dama-uk.org/resources/the-six-primary-dimensions-for-data-quality-assessment)); DCAM is limited to EDM Council members/Authorized Partners ([EDM Council](https://edmcouncil.org/frameworks/dcam/)).
- Mandatory contexts: LEI mandates (GLEIF list above); ISO/IEC 27001 Clauses 4–10 non-excludable for conformity ([IEC abstract](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/79694)); FIPS 184 federal applicability with waiver procedure ([FIPS 184 §12](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/FIPS/fipspub184.pdf)), withdrawn 2008 (Wikipedia, search).

### Inferences
- A framework can cite standards by number without purchasing them, using the public scope statements, but must avoid asserting clause content; where clause-level precision is needed (e.g., 11179-5 naming rules, 25012 definitions), the framework should either buy the standard or cite a conformant public registry's documentation (e.g., METeOR, caDSR) as the operationalisation.

### Gaps
- Prices for ISO/IEC 9075 parts, ISO/IEC 11179-3/-5 and ISO 3166/4217/639 documents were not visible (IEC webstore showed "CHF 0.-" for 11179-5, likely an artefact).
